I lead Solutions Engineering at a large software company, and I’ve spent 10+ years inside B2B SaaS running Sales Engineering, Solutions Architecture, Implementation, and Technical Account Management—from the Fortune 500 down to Series A startups.

Here’s what that job actually is, stripped of the sales context: sit with a business, map how the work really flows, translate the pain into a technical design, build a proof of concept with success criteria and a deadline, and demonstrate it working on the buyer’s own data—to an audience that is paid to be skeptical. Then make sure what was promised is what gets delivered, adopted, and maintained.

That is, word for word, the job of a good automation consultant. The only difference is who pays: vendors used to pay me to prove their software worked. Now clients pay me to make automation work for them. A decade of running proof-of-concept evaluations is a decade of being professionally punished for over-promising—which is precisely the instinct missing from most of the AI consulting market.

Proofworks is where I put that to work. Not AI transformation decks—specific automations for the manual work that’s eating your team’s week, each one piloted against written success criteria before you spend real money scaling it. Built properly, documented, and owned by you.

Operating Principles

How I Work

What clients can expect from every engagement

1

Proof over promises

Success criteria are written down before anything gets built. The pilot either hits them or you stop. I win by being right, not by being convincing.

2

I build, not just advise

You don’t get a slide deck and a wave goodbye. You get working automations wired into the tools your team actually uses.

3

Boring beats clever

Sometimes the right answer is an AI agent; sometimes it’s a well-placed Zapier zap or a form. I recommend the cheapest thing that reliably works—even when it’s unglamorous.

4

Humans stay in the loop

Automation drafts, routes, and processes; people approve where judgment or risk lives. The goal is leverage for your team, not a robot running unsupervised.

5

Your team owns it

Everything is documented and handed over—architecture, credentials, runbooks. No black boxes, no dependency designed to keep you paying.

6

Measured or it didn’t happen

Hours returned, turnaround time, error rates—every automation reports its own impact. If we can’t measure it, we don’t claim it.

Why This, Why Now

The Technology Finally Caught Up to the Work

For most of my career, the honest answer to “can we automate this?” was “only the rigid parts.” Traditional automation could move data between systems, but the moment a task required reading a document, understanding a request, or drafting a response, it needed a person. So businesses hired coordinators, admins, and ops staff to be the glue between their tools.

That constraint is gone. Modern AI handles exactly the messy middle—reading the invoice regardless of format, understanding the client email, drafting the proposal from the call notes. Combined with solid process design and integration engineering, the workflows that used to require a hire can now run as systems.

What hasn’t changed is that most AI projects still fail the way enterprise software projects always failed: built on a guess, measured by enthusiasm, abandoned quietly. The fix isn’t better technology. It’s the discipline evaluation-driven buyers have always forced on vendors—success criteria first, proof on real data, then scale.

I’ve spent a decade on the receiving end of that discipline. Now it works for you.
