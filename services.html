Illustrative Scenarios

What an Engagement Looks Like

Two illustrative examples—composites of the pattern, not client case studies. Problem → Pilot → What changes.

The Sales Team That Stopped Doing Data Entry

B2B SaaS · ~40 people · 6-person sales team · illustrative

Problem

Reps spent the last hour of every day updating the CRM from memory—or didn’t. Pipeline reviews ran on stale data, follow-ups slipped, and proposals took three days because every one started from a blank page.

Pilot

Thirty-day pilot, two automations: call recordings summarized into CRM fields with follow-up tasks created, and proposal first-drafts generated from call context plus their best past proposals. Success criteria: CRM updated within 1 hour of every call, proposal turnaround under 1 day.

What Changes

  • CRM reflects reality without anyone typing
  • Proposals go out same-day, reviewed not written
  • Each rep gets 4–6 selling hours back per week
  • Scorecard passes → rolls into a partnership

The Services Firm That Got Its Fridays Back

Professional services firm · ~20 people · no in-house engineering · illustrative

Problem

Client intake meant re-keying the same information into five places. Invoices went out late, collections meant awkward manual emails the partners kept postponing, and month-end reporting consumed two full days of the office manager’s time.

Pilot

Thirty-day pilot on intake: one form feeding every downstream system, engagement letters drafted automatically, files and folders created, kickoff scheduled. Success criteria: intake time from 3 hours to under 20 minutes, zero re-keying. Collections and reporting queued as cycles two and three.

What Changes

  • New clients onboarded the day they sign
  • The office manager stops being the human API
  • A repeatable pattern for the next two automations
  • Growth stops meaning “hire another coordinator”

FAQ

Fair Questions, Straight Answers

What happens if the pilot fails?+

You stop, and you keep everything: the working build, the documentation, and a written analysis of why it missed. That’s the point of the structure—a failed pilot costs a fraction of a failed transformation project, and you learn something real either way. Roughly speaking, if I don’t think a pilot can pass, I’ll tell you at the audit stage and we won’t run one.

Is this going to replace people on my team?+

In practice, it replaces the worst parts of their jobs. The businesses I work with aren’t trying to shrink—they’re trying to grow without every new client adding coordination overhead. Automation absorbs the re-keying, chasing, and assembling; your people do the judgment work they were actually hired for.

What tools do you build with?+

Whatever the problem actually needs—which is a real answer, not a dodge. Sometimes that’s an AI model reading documents and drafting responses; sometimes it’s workflow tools like Zapier, Make, or n8n; sometimes it’s custom code against your systems’ APIs. I’m deliberately not a reseller for any platform, so the recommendation is never shaped by a commission.

What about our data? Some of it is sensitive.+

Data handling is designed per engagement, in writing: what data the automation touches, where it’s processed, what leaves your systems (often nothing), and which AI providers, if any, see it under what terms. I’ve spent years walking enterprise security teams through exactly these reviews—yours will be the easiest version of that conversation I’ve ever had.

Couldn’t we just do this ourselves with Zapier and ChatGPT?+

Maybe—and if so, the audit will tell you exactly that, with a roadmap your own team can execute. The hard part usually isn’t the tools; it’s knowing which process to automate first, where the edge cases hide, what to leave manual, and how to make it survive month two. That’s process engineering, and it’s the part I sell.

Do we need clean data or documented processes first?+

No. Almost nobody has them, and waiting for them is how automation stays a someday-project forever. The audit works from how things actually run—including the messy parts—and the roadmap accounts for what needs cleaning up along the way. Perfect documentation is an output of this work, not a prerequisite.

What does this cost?+

Every engagement is a fixed fee scoped on the intro call, and every proposal includes the ROI math—hours returned times what those hours cost you—so you can judge the price against a number, not a feeling. Audits are the smallest commitment, pilots are mid-range and creditable toward a partnership, and partnerships are monthly with a three-month initial term.
