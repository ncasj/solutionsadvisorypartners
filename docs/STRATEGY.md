# Proofworks — Brand Strategy & Narrative

*The strategic foundation for the pivot from fractional Solutions Engineering consulting
(Solutions Advisory Partners) to an AI automation consultancy (Proofworks).*

---

## 1. The Pivot in One Paragraph

Solutions Advisory Partners sold **a category almost nobody searches for** (fractional
solutions engineering) to **a narrow ICP** (10–50 person software companies with technical
sales motions) with **a pitch that required education** ("here's what an SE even is, and why
you're missing one"). The pivot inverts all three: **AI automation consulting** is a category
buyers are actively searching for and budgeting for right now; the ICP widens to any
operations-heavy business, not just software companies with demo-heavy sales; and the SE
background stops being the product and becomes the **evidence** — the reason to trust this
particular consultant in a market flooded with prompt-jockeys who have never run a
production system or sat in front of a skeptical enterprise buyer.

## 2. Positioning Statement

> **Proofworks is an AI automation consultancy for growing businesses that are done with AI
> hype.** Led by a practicing enterprise Solutions Engineering leader, every engagement
> follows the discipline of a technical sales evaluation: define success criteria up front,
> prove the automation on your own data in a 30-day pilot, and only then scale it. You never
> buy a promise — you buy something you've already watched work.

**Category:** AI automation consulting (audit → pilot → managed automation)
**For:** founders, owners, and ops leaders of 10–200 person businesses
**Unlike:** AI agencies selling transformation decks, dev shops selling billable hours, and
DIY tools that die in a drawer
**Because:** the proof-first method — borrowed directly from a decade of running enterprise
POCs — de-risks every dollar

## 3. The Narrative Bridge (why an SE is *more* credible here, not less)

This is the single most important story the site must tell. The pivot only works if the SE
past reads as qualification, not baggage. The bridge:

**What a Solutions Engineer actually does, stripped of the sales context:**

1. **Discovery** — sit with a business, map how work actually flows, find where it hurts
2. **Solution mapping** — translate business pain into a technical design, honestly scoped
3. **Proof** — build a POC with agreed success criteria and a clock, then demonstrate it
   working on the buyer's own data against a skeptical technical audience
4. **Handoff** — make sure what was promised is what gets delivered, adopted, and maintained

That is, word for word, the job of a good automation consultant. The difference is only who
pays: as an SE, the vendor pays you to prove software works; as a consultant, the client
pays you to make software work for them. Ten years of enterprise POCs is ten years of
**being professionally punished for over-promising** — the exact instinct the AI consulting
market is missing.

**One-liner versions of the bridge (use in hero copy, LinkedIn, intros):**

- "I spent ten years proving software to skeptical buyers. Now I apply that standard to AI."
- "Solutions Engineers are the people vendors send in when the buyer says 'prove it.'
  That's the energy your AI project needs."
- "Every AI pitch you've heard is a demo. I've run a decade of POCs — I know the difference."

Nico Castagnola — boutique and senior is a *feature* for this ICP ("you get the person on
the website").

## 5. The Branded Method: Proof-First

Four stages, used everywhere (services page, proposals, LinkedIn content):

1. **Map** — a paid two-week audit: inventory the manual work, score effort vs. impact,
   attach dollar values. Deliverable: a ranked automation roadmap you own either way.
2. **Pilot** — pick the top one or two automations. Define success criteria *in writing*
   (hours saved, turnaround time, error rate). Build in ~30 days on your real data.
3. **Prove** — measure against the criteria. Hit → scale. Miss → you stop, having spent a
   fraction of what an agency retainer costs. The kill-switch is the point.
4. **Scale** — roll out, train the team, document everything, then compound: one new
   automation at a time under a monthly partnership.

**Why this is the differentiator:** every competitor sells confidence. Proofworks sells
*evidence*, and structurally accepts the risk of a failed pilot. That asymmetry is very hard
for hype-driven competitors to copy, because their economics depend on long engagements.

## 6. Messaging by Audience Temperature

- **Cold (site visitors, SEO):** anti-hype. "You've seen the demos. Here's what actually
  ships." Concrete example automations with plain-language descriptions and honest caveats.
- **Warm (LinkedIn network — mostly SaaS GTM people):** the career-story angle. "I've spent
  ten years watching what survives a POC and what dies in one. Applying that to AI
  automation for real businesses. Here's this week's build." Build-in-public content.
- **Hot (discovery calls):** ROI math, live walkthroughs of automations built for similar
  businesses, pilot scoping. The assessment score is the warm-up conversation.

## 8. Voice & Tone

- First person singular. "I build…" — never fake-plural.
- Plain words over jargon; when a technical term is needed, define it in the sentence.
- Specific over superlative: "an intake automation that saved a 9-person firm ~11
  hours/week" beats "transformative efficiency gains."
- Calm about AI. No breathless futurism, no doomer contrarianism. The tone of an engineer
  who has seen many hype cycles: *some of this is real, let's find your part of it.*
- Every claim either provable or labeled illustrative.
