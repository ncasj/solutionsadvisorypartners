# Proofworks — ICP & Go-to-Market Strategy

---

## 1. ICP: Beachhead and Expansion

### Beachhead ICP (first 6 months): B2B SaaS & tech-enabled companies — *where the network already is*

The pivot changes the **service**, not the network. Ten years in B2B SaaS means the warm
audience — founders, sales leaders, RevOps, CS leaders — is concentrated there. They are
also the easiest AI-automation buyers in the market right now: they already believe, they
just haven't operationalized.

- **Firmographics:** B2B SaaS / tech-enabled services, 10–100 employees, $1M–$20M ARR,
  post-revenue, US (NYC overweight)
- **Economic buyer:** Founder/CEO or COO. Champion: RevOps, Head of CS, or Ops manager.
- **Pains to lead with:** CRM hygiene and data entry; call notes → CRM; proposal/quote
  drafting; RFP & security questionnaires; lead intake, enrichment & routing; customer
  onboarding handoffs; renewal/QBR prep; support triage; investor/board reporting packs
- **Why they buy:** headcount discipline is permanent post-2023; "do more with the team we
  have" is board-level doctrine.

### Expansion ICP (months 4+): operations-heavy traditional SMBs

- **Firmographics:** professional services (accounting, legal, consulting), agencies,
  wholesale/distribution, specialty healthcare admin, financial services boutiques;
  10–200 employees, $2M–$50M revenue; owner-led
- **Economic buyer:** Owner/founder or COO; champion is the office/ops manager who is
  drowning.
- **Pains:** intake and scheduling; invoicing, AP coding, collections chasing; document
  processing; client status communication; monthly reporting; the owner as approval
  bottleneck
- **Why they're attractive:** far less competition from talented consultants, deep
  gratitude and referrals, sticky retainers. Reachable through referral partners
  (fractional CFOs/COOs, MSPs, bookkeepers, agencies) rather than content.

### Explicit non-ICP (say no)

Pre-revenue startups wanting a technical cofounder at consulting prices; enterprises with
internal automation teams (that's a job, not a client); anyone shopping for "an AI
strategy deck" with no intent to ship; regulated workflows where an error is catastrophic
and no human review loop is acceptable.

## 2. Offer Ladder & Pricing

Website shows structure and fixed-fee framing; exact numbers live in proposals. Guidance:

| Rung | Offer | Scope | Price guidance | Job |
|------|-------|-------|----------------|-----|
| 0 | **Automation Readiness Assessment** (free, on-site) | 10-question self-serve score | $0 | Lead capture + call starter |
| 1 | **Automation Audit** | 2 weeks: process inventory, effort/impact scoring, ROI math, ranked roadmap | $4,500–$7,500 fixed | Paid discovery; de-risks both sides; the roadmap sells the pilot |
| 2 | **Pilot** ("prove it") | ~30 days: build 1–2 automations against written success criteria, measured | $8,000–$20,000 fixed | The flagship. Go/no-go gate is the brand promise. |
| 3 | **Automation Partner** | Monthly: run + maintain + one new automation per cycle, team enablement, office hours | $3,000–$10,000/mo, 3-mo initial term | Recurring revenue engine; target 4–6 accounts max |
| — | **Advisory variant** | Design + governance while client's devs build | ~60% of build pricing | For teams with engineering capacity |

**Pricing rules:** value-anchored (every proposal shows hours saved × loaded rate),
fixed-fee over hourly (SEs know how to scope), pilot fee creditable toward the first
partner month, no long contracts — the proof is the retention mechanism.

**Founding-client motion (first 90 days):** 2–3 engagements at ~40% discount in exchange
for a written case study with real numbers and a referral intro. Real case studies are the
single biggest asset gap; buy them with margin, not with free work.

## 3. Channels, Ranked

1. **Warm network activation (weeks 1–2).** Personal note to 50 best-fit contacts: not
   "I'm rebranding," but "I'm taking 3 founding clients to automate X/Y/Z — who do you
   know?" Asking for referrals positions the news as scarcity, not need.
2. **LinkedIn build-in-public (ongoing, 2–3 posts/wk).** The SE→AI narrative post (pinned),
   then weekly teardown posts: one real automation, what it replaced, hours saved, what
   broke, honest caveats. Anti-hype voice is the moat; screenshots and short screen
   recordings outperform text.
3. **Referral partners (month 2+).** Fractional CFOs/COOs, MSPs, bookkeeping firms,
   agencies serving the same SMBs. Offer 10% referral fee or reciprocal referrals. This is
   the primary channel into the expansion ICP.
4. **Assessment as lead magnet.** Promote in every post footer and email signature; score
   email triggers a personal follow-up within 24h.
5. **SEO/content (compounding, slow).** The four launch articles target automation-intent
   queries ("AI automation consultant", "business process automation for small business",
   "AI pilot success criteria"). One new article/month minimum.
6. **NYC in-person (month 2+).** Founder/ops meetups; offer a free 20-min "automation
   audit live" teardown talk — the talk is the funnel.

## 4. 90-Day Plan

**Days 1–14 — Launch.** New site live on new domain; LinkedIn profile + company page
renamed/created; pinned narrative post; personal notes to 50 contacts; Calendly + EmailJS +
Mixpanel re-pointed; old domain 301s to new.

**Days 15–45 — Founding clients.** Close 2–3 founding engagements (audit or pilot);
publish weekly build-in-public posts from that work; first referral-partner conversations;
target: 10 discovery calls, 2 paid engagements.

**Days 46–90 — Proof inventory.** First case study with real numbers published; first
pilot → partner conversion; second wave of outreach using the case study; first expansion-ICP
engagement via referral partner; target: pipeline of 5+, MRR ≥ $6k.

## 5. Metrics That Matter

- Discovery calls / month (target 8–10 by day 60)
- Audit → Pilot conversion (healthy: >60% — if lower, audits are finding weak ROI or the
  roadmap isn't selling)
- Pilot success-criteria hit rate (this **is** the brand; publish it when it's strong)
- Pilot → Partner conversion (healthy: >50%)
- MRR from Partner retainers; case studies published (leading indicator of everything)

## 6. Positioning Against Alternatives (objection cribsheet)

| Alternative | Their pitch | The counter |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| AI agency | "AI transformation roadmap" | "Ask them what happens if the pilot fails. My answer is in the contract." |
| Dev shop | hourly builds | "They bill effort. I commit to criteria." |
| Zapier/DIY | "$30/mo and a weekend" | "Great for simple triggers — I'll tell you in the audit if that's all you need. The expensive part is knowing *what* to build and what to leave manual." |
| Hire someone | full-time automation/ops hire | "$120k+ and six months to learn your business. A pilot costs a fraction and tells you what the hire should even work on." |
| Do nothing | "we're managing" | The assessment + audit ROI math exists precisely for this. Cost of manual work is invisible until priced. |
